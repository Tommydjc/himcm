{\Large\bfseries AeroTherm Home Climate System Ends Manual Thermostat Wars}\\[0.35em]
{\large\itshape New controller shifts cooling before peak rates and
slashes needless compressor starts---even on a brutal summer day}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.7em}
\noindent
BOSTON, MA --- October 25, 2018 --- GoHiMCM today unveiled
\textbf{AeroTherm}, a next-generation home climate system designed for
real life: late trains, surprise returns, and households that never agree
on the thermostat.

\medskip
\noindent
Programmable thermostats still demand fixed schedules.
Many ``learning'' devices also stumble when routines break or roommates
clash.
AeroTherm treats the house walls as a thermal battery.
It pre-cools before expensive afternoon rates, then eases off when prices spike.
When you open the door, the room is already in the comfort band.

\medskip
\noindent
The system also treats air as more than temperature.
It can track humidity and airborne irritants such as pollen and fine particles.
It keeps indoor humidity away from the high-moisture zone where dust mites
and mold thrive.
A medical-grade filtration mode raises purification when outdoor air is
dirty, instead of pulling unfiltered air indoors.

\medskip
\noindent
In a locked 24-hour summer teaching benchmark against a classic on/off
thermostat, AeroTherm cut the utility bill by \textbf{4.2\%} and
on-peak cooling energy by \textbf{15.8\%}.
Compressor starts fell from 42 to 3.
Peak-period electricity share dropped from 43.2\% of the day's bill to 38.0\%.
Mean indoor temperature stayed near $23^\circ\mathrm{C}$.

\medskip
\noindent
``A truly smart home should not force you to live like a clockwork robot
just to save on utility bills,'' said Dr.\ Maya Chen, AeroTherm chief
scientist.
``Families deserve comfort that arrives with them---and air that stays
safe for kids with allergies.''

\medskip
\noindent
For larger homes, AeroTherm zones rooms with smart dampers.
Empty rooms become gentle thermal buffers instead of wasted full cooling.
Household preference conflicts are settled by a fairness bargain that
gives extra weight to allergy-sensitive residents.
